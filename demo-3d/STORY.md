# Snowed In — the story (3D version)

The 3D game keeps the shape of the 2D story (an ordinary shared day, something
strange, an imitation, and a choice between a perfect day that never ends and
real time that passes) but it is no longer about a birthday. The anchor is
**the first snow**: the night the two of them met.

## Premise

They met as children at a winter lantern fair, on the night of the first snow,
when both of them wandered off from the crowd. Neither remembers much of it:
snow, lanterns, somebody small sitting on a log. *You came over. That part,
everyone agrees on.*

Every year since, on the day of the first real snow, they keep a small
tradition: a walk, a cake with one candle for every first snow in the cabin,
paper lanterns on the porch, and the three-note whistle they made up at the
fair, *if one of us gets lost*. They call each other Partner, and they speak a
little formally, warmly, with a lot of dry teasing underneath.

The question underneath the whole story: they have forgotten almost
everything about how they began, and it has never mattered. What if something
offered to remember all of it for them, forever, as long as nothing ever
changed again?

## Act I · Shared Life — the first snow day

- **Prologue.** The lantern fair, as children (their child selves are the same
  characters, younger). Music from a music box somewhere among the stalls. You
  can take a paper lantern from the lantern man and buy one small bag of
  roasted chestnuts before going to find the girl on the log, who is watching
  the snow, the fair, her own boots. *"Are you lost?" / "Is this seat taken?"
  "It's a log."* / or just sitting down. The lantern stood in the snow, the
  chestnuts split exactly in half (she checks). Her nose is pink and she has no
  scarf; you can give her yours. Sky lanterns go up from the fair and over
  them; a wish, out loud or secret. She makes up the three-note whistle, *if I
  get lost*, and you whistle it back, make your own, or get it wrong. Someone
  calls her name: *"Will you be here tomorrow?"* A promise (or a little
  finger). You walk her back to the lanterns, hand in hand. White-out. *(done)*
  Later the day only half-remembers it: the snow globe's tune sounds like
  somewhere else for a moment; she has *a feeling* that scarf was hers once,
  for about an hour; a wish feels like one made before. Neither of them is
  ever sure.
- **Morning.** Snowed in. The bed, the blanket, pancakes, tea, breakfast with
  its choices, the house and its small stories. Partner behaves as if it's an
  ordinary snow day and keeps slipping into the locked study ("private"). An
  envelope on the table is marked "not yet". *(done)*
- **The walk.** Out into the fresh snow: a pinecone contest in the meadow, a
  race up the hill to the bench ("Do you think we're the same people we were
  when we were kids?"), sliding stones on the frozen lake. Then the **summer
  circle**: a perfect ring of green grass and wildflowers in the snow, warm
  air, and two sets of children's footprints coming from opposite sides and
  stopping face to face. Partner is frightened; they go home hand in hand at
  sunset. *(done)*

Around the story, the cabin is lived in: coats, hats and boots by the door
(she wears the cream bobble hat she had at the fair; he still has the red
scarf), the cold creeping in if you stay out too long, a fire that burns down
and a woodpile on the porch, weather that comes and goes, and Partner's own
routines when you leave her to it (reading in the armchair until she dozes
off, watering Basil and the herbs, the radio). Things to do together: snowball
fights, a snowman (the sad lemon from the fridge becomes his nose), snow
angels, cocoa, reading aloud on the sofa, cards at the kitchen table (there is
a queen up her sleeve), and photographs that end up on the fridge.

## Act II · Something Strange — the evening

- Home at dusk, every window lit. Partner tries hard to be normal.
- The reveal she kept all day: she hadn't forgotten what day it is. The cake,
  five candles, a wish. *(done, for now as the evening's first beat)*
- The empty frame in the hall now holds a photograph: two children in the
  snow, lanterns blurred behind them (whoever ended up with the red scarf at
  the fair is wearing it). Neither remembers it being taken. They compare what
  they remember: the colour of the scarf (she says blue), how many trees (she
  says three), the first thing either of them said (she says "nice hat"). If you
  pick what you really said in the prologue, you know it's right without
  knowing how. They agree on almost nothing, except the whistle: three notes,
  and you whistle it back the way you did as a child. Afterwards the lanterns
  in the photograph look a little brighter. *(done)*
- Lanterns on the porch: nine paper lanterns from the box by the door, lit one
  by one. Her present from the study: a snow globe of the night they met, with
  a cinnamon-stick log. *"Seven. I didn't remember how many trees there were,
  so I guessed."* (*"You said three, earlier." "Earlier I was remembering. Now
  I'm guessing."*) *(done)*
- Watching the stars on the bench. Then something that should not be there in
  winter: a firefly, drifting down through the falling snow past the lanterns.
  *"It's the first snow, Partner. There aren't any fireflies."* Her hand finds
  yours. *"Inside. Please."* *(done)*

## Act III · Recognition — the night *(done: rebuilt to follow the 2D Entity rules)*

**Bedtime (the real her).** In bed after the porch: "A good first snow?" (top three / apart from the firefly / every one with you is), then hold, look at or kiss her. The plant line: *"If I get up for water, don't steal my side." / "You don't recognise sides." / "…Touché. Goodnight, giant."* Lamps out.

**The night (the imitation).** You wake cold; her side is empty. Footsteps. She comes back: "Couldn't sleep. Got water." She lies down exactly on her side. "Come back to sleep." Then you're free.
- **Go back to sleep beside it**: corruption; you wake to the whistle very close and her eyes open.
- **Get up**: after ~10 s it gets up too and follows you ("Can't sleep either?"). Talk to it anywhere: cocoa, the last of the fire, the window seat, the last of the cake, **tease**, or "Who are you?". It answers almost right: it repeats this morning's lines word for word, gives up a joke line, doesn't flinch when poked, never checks the cake halves, pushes its chair in square, and is certain about scores and the night you met. Agreeing with its certainties or sitting at its fire adds corruption.
- **Social stealth**: staring at it for 5 s up close ("…What?"), running near it, or confronting it adds suspicion. At 3 it goes back to bed and lies with its eyes open.
- **The whistle**: the real one's, from the kitchen, after 14 s and then every 18–26 s. The first time it says "…Pipes. Old house." After two whistles a **door** appears in the kitchen wall. There's a hint after 70 s.
- **Optional, noticed if you look**: three mugs on the rack, the photo's third shape, snow inside the bedroom sill. The front door won't open.

**Elsewhere (played as her).** Through the door (black if you slept beside it, pale blue if not), the bodies swap: "Earlier." She's up for water. Something in his shape walks all the way into the kitchen: "Come back to bed, Partner." (He never comes all the way into the kitchen.) Three replies, each with a tell. It leaves without waiting. You whistle at the window, leave a sign (pinecones / pancake bite / note), and walk out of the front door into the snow. Then the "Act IV · The Same Day" card and the end card.

**The Entity's notes.** `G.observe` logs what it learns about how you play (its own voice, in the console) and keeps the last 80 in this browser under `snowed.entity.observations`. This is ready for Act IV.

## Act IV · The Same Day

- A perfect copy of today, the first snow forever: perfect pancakes nobody
  steals from, a perfect cabin, a perfect Partner who remembers the night at
  the fair in exact detail. The real one never could.
- Three notes, whistled, far off to the east. Follow them. The real Partner
  is under the big tree in the snow. *"What did I say, the first time?" "I have
  no idea. I never did."*
- Getting back together: a fallen tree you can only cross by helping each
  other, ice that gives way, holding on.
- A door standing on its own in the snow. The thing that kept all this speaks:
  *"You could stay. It could always be the first snow. Nobody would get older.
  Nothing would be forgotten. Not the pancakes, not the hill, not the snow.
  I would remember it for you."* Stay, or go home.

## Endings

Your choices across the day (bond, trust, rivalry, how often you accepted the
imitation, whether you noticed its flaws) decide which you reach.

- **The Same Day** — you stay. It is lovely. It is always the same.
- **The Perfect Partner** — you went into the perfect house with the imitation.
- **I Know It's You** — home, but you can't quite stop checking.
- **Uncertain Morning** — home, and neither of you is entirely sure.
- **Found Me** — home, together, certain, with nothing remembered and nothing
  missing.
- **The Thaw** (the true ending, on a second run that remembers the first):
  the thing explains that it remembers everything you have forgotten, and
  offers it all back. You choose to keep forgetting, and keep going. Morning:
  snow sliding off the roof, water dripping from the eaves. Spring coming.

The closing card is set in the config (`closing`): by default,
*"Here's to another winter."*

## What changed from the 2D game

- No birthday: the day is the first snow, the anniversary of the night they
  met. The cake, the snow globe and the "not yet" envelope are about that.
- The seasons are flipped: winter outside, and the wrong-season sign is summer
  (the grass circle, the firefly) instead of snow on a summer day.
- They call each other Partner; the register is gentler and a little formal.
- *The Last Birthday* becomes *The Same Day*; *The First Snow* ending becomes
  *The Thaw*.
- The 2D game itself is unchanged and still tells the birthday version.
